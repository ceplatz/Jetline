How to become a pilot

The EASA roadmap, simplified.

Europe focuses on intensive training that can put you in a jet cockpit with as few as 200 hours. It is a marathon, so plan time, discipline and budget.

1 · Check the basics
  • Medical: Class 1 at an EASA aero-medical centre.
  • Education: high school diploma. Maths and physics help.
  • Age: start at any age, 21 for an ATPL.
2 · Modular steps

PPL, night VFR, time building, ATPL theory (distance learning), multi-engine and CPL, then multi-engine instrument rating and final exam.

3 · Multi-crew + type rating

MCC and your A320 type rating make you job-ready for airline selection.

4 · Mentoring and simulators

Experienced pilots mentor you throughout, and our simulators let you practise before you fly.

What the job offers

Airline pilot salaries are high relative to other professions in Europe or Asia, with strong fringe benefits and growth with experience. A global fleet means a global career.

ICAO to EASA

Convert your licence.

We convert third-country ICAO licences to an EASA Part-FCL licence issued by Swiss FOCA. It is not an automatic exchange, but it can be done in 8 to 10 weeks.

CPL conversion

Pass ATPL theory, 200+ hours total (100 PIC, a 300 NM cross-country), a 5–15 hour bespoke conversion course, then two CPL skill tests on the Tecnam P2006T.

from EUR 7,300

ATPL conversion

For airline pilots: 1,500+ hours incl. 500 on multi-pilot aircraft, ATPL theory and an ATPL skill test in the full flight simulator.

from EUR 2,950
First steps for every candidate
  • EASA medical (Class 1 for CPL/ATPL, Class 2 for PPL) at an aero-medical centre. Get it before you invest in training.
  • ICAO English level 4 via a 45-minute online assessment with our language assessors.
  • Licence verification from your State of Licence Issue.
  • Choose Swiss FOCA as your competent authority and have us audit your logbooks. Start theory early: it takes 2–6 months.
